# Lamination

Folding a sheet of butter into dough and rolling and folding it repeatedly to build thin alternating layers. The steam from the butter's water lifts the layers in the oven ([[laminated-dough]]).

## How we do it

- Butter block pounded to a 1 cm sheet (Glenvarrow blocks) or used as delivered (Coldbeck plates, trial only).
- Three single folds, 30 minutes in the chiller between each.
- Dough and butter at the same plasticity: butter around 12 to 15 °C, dough cold. If the butter cracks it is too cold; if it smears it is too warm.
- About 45 g of butter in each croissant once dough butter is counted ([[croissant-recipe-card]]).

## Why it is in the cost story

Lamination is where the butter goes, so it is where butter quality and butter price both show. A cheaper butter that smears costs more in failed batches than it saves; see [[all-butter-croissants-are-not-where-we-save]] and the Coldbeck test in [[priyanka-email-butter-sheet-trial-2026-10]].

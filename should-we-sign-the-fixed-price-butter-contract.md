# Should We Sign The Fixed-Price Butter Contract?

## Why It Matters

Butter is the biggest single cost in our viennoiserie and has gone from 6.20 to 8.90 EUR/kg in 21 months ([[butter-price-tracker]]). November to April is our heaviest butter period. Whatever we sign decides whether the croissant needs another price move in spring 2027 ([[can-the-croissant-stay-at-2-95-eur]]).

## Current Answer

> [!question]
> Not decided. Leaning towards a fixed price for six months, from whichever dairy is cheaper on paper and passes a second lamination test, because the volume commitment is safe and a known price makes the spring costing simple.

## Options

| Option | Price | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glenvarrow fixed | 8.40 for 900 kg, Nov to Apr | Reliable, daily delivery, milk on the same van | Shortfall charge; pays over market if butter falls |
| Coldbeck fixed | "around 8.20", quote due 2026-10-30 | Possibly cheaper; plates save Priyanka 40 minutes a week | One test only; twice-weekly delivery; new relationship |
| Stay monthly | 8.90 now, list | No commitment | Moira expects 9.00 to 9.20 by spring |

## What Would Settle It

- Coldbeck's written price on a comparable commitment.
- A second lamination test on a warm morning ([[priyanka-email-butter-sheet-trial-2026-10]]).
- Whether splitting butter from milk and cream would sour things with Glenvarrow. Moira says it would not; I half believe her.

Deadline: Moira's 2026-11-06. Owner: me. Ruth's general advice applies: buy certainty if it is cheap, do not bet on prices.

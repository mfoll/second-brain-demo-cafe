# Gross Profit Margin

How I use gross margin at Hollowgate: what is in it, what is not, and the targets each part of the business has to meet.

## Definition

> [!info]
> Gross margin is sales minus the cost of the ingredients and packaging that went into them, as a percentage of sales, both net of VAT. Labour is not in it. A croissant selling at 2.95 EUR (2.68 EUR net of VAT) with 0.91 EUR of ingredients (September 2026 butter) has a gross margin of 66%.

The textbook term is in [[gross-margin]]. I calculate it at three levels:

- **Product**: from the [[recipe-costing-sheet]], ingredient cost per item against net price. Recosted when an ingredient moves more than 5% (butter, see [[butter-price-tracker]]).
- **Menu section**: bakery, savoury lunch, drinks, wholesale, from Xero sales by account against purchases, monthly ([[margin-by-menu-section-2025]]).
- **Whole shop**: the year's number in the accounts.

## Targets

| Level | Target | 2025 actual |
|---|---|---|
| Whole shop | 68% | 69.4% |
| Bakery counter | 70% | 71% |
| Savoury lunch | 60% | 51% |
| Drinks | 75% | 78% |
| Wholesale bread | 35% | 33% |

Wholesale is lower by design: no VAT-inclusive retail price, no counter staff, and the customer collects. The 35% floor was set in June 2025 when [[wholesale-to-cafes]] started.

## How I Use It

- Menu decisions: the 20-point gap between bakery (71%) and lunch (51%) drove the cut at [[team-meeting-2026-02-05]]. The partial reversal at [[menu-review-2026-04-09]] showed the limit: margin is not takings, and losing lunch customers cost bakery and coffee sales too.
- Prices: the [[price-review-2026]] aimed to restore 68% overall after butter rose.
- Second site: the unit 7 model assumes 66%, two points under Hollowgate ([[saltings-hall-three-scenario-model]]).

## Limits

Gross margin ignores labour, which is where lunch really lost money: 30 cook hours a week. For that I use [[prime-cost]] and [[contribution-margin]]. It also says nothing about cash timing ([[cash-buffer]]).

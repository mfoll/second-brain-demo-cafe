# How Do We Cool Cooked Fillings Safely

How should the bakery cool cooked fillings and soup so that they pass through the warm range fast enough, with a record that proves it?

## Current Answer

> [!info]
> Cool in shallow trays or containers no deeper than 5 cm, uncovered, on the rack by the extractor, and record the core temperature at the end of cooking, at 2 hours (below 20 °C) and at 6 hours (below 5 °C). Discard any batch that misses either stage. Soup goes into four shallow containers, never a deep pot.

## Why it was asked

We started cooking fillings in March 2025. The plan named cooling as a critical control point but the sheet had no line for it, and the June inspection marked us down ([[environmental-health-inspection-2025-06-18]]).

## Evidence

- Trials in June 2025: ham and leek filling in a 5 cm tray reached 20 °C in 65 to 75 minutes; the same batch in a deep pot took over three hours.
- Soup in a 15-litre pot was still at 38 °C after 2 hours. In four shallow containers it reached 19 °C at 1 h 50.
- An ice bath works for soup in a rush but takes space we do not have.
- Background: [[two-stage-cooling]], [[clostridium-perfringens]].

## Rejected options

- A blast chiller: about 4,500 EUR and no room in the basement. Revisit only if volumes double, for example with a [[central-production-kitchen]].

# Break-Even Month

The month in which a new venture's cumulative cash, including what was spent to open it, first gets back to zero. Not the month it first makes a monthly profit.

## Why I Use It

[[farid-nasser]] made the distinction on the finance course: a new site can be "profitable" from month 5 and still not have paid back its fit-out until month 14. The textbook break-even point ([[break-even-point]]) gives a turnover; the break-even month gives a date, which is what the bank, the forecast and my nerves care about.

## In Practice

For unit 7 ([[saltings-hall-three-scenario-model]]):

- Monthly profit turns positive in month 4 in the base case.
- Cumulative cash including the 120,000 EUR investment gets back to zero in month 14.
- In the low case it never does within 36 months.

Moving costs between sites changes it: [[ruth-marchbank]] moved the loan repayments into the unit's costs and the base case moved from month 12 to 14.

## Caveat

It depends on what counts as the investment. I include working capital; some people do not, which flatters the result by four or five months.

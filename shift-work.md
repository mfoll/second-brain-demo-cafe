# Shift work

## Summary

Wikipedia's article on shift work: dividing the day into set periods so that a service or production line keeps running, including fixed night shifts, early shifts and rotating patterns. It covers how shift systems are designed and the health and safety concerns linked to working against the body clock, such as poor sleep, fatigue and the accidents and errors that follow.

## Why It Matters

I read it the weekend after the [[early-shift-overtime-review-2025-09-02]], when the Deputy hours showed Kofi at 47 hours a week on 04:30 starts. Our early shift is not a night shift, but the sleep side applies: people starting at 04:30 go to bed early and lose evenings. It shaped two things: the rule that nobody is rostered for an early shift straight after a closing shift ([[rota-principles]]), and my reading of Kofi's objection to 03:00 starts for a [[central-production-kitchen]] as a serious one, not a preference.

## Key Details

- Shift design choices (fixed or rotating, length, rest between shifts) matter as much as total hours.
- Fatigue raises the risk of mistakes and injuries.
- Very early and night work disrupt sleep in ways that do not fully adapt.

## Caveats

General workforce research summarised at encyclopedia level; it does not give us a number. Our 42 and 45-hour lines are judgement ([[no-one-should-routinely-work-more-than-45-hours]]).

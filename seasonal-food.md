# Seasonal food

## Summary

Short article on seasonal food: eating produce when its harvest or flavour peaks, usually at harvest though not always (it notes sweet potatoes improve weeks later). It links seasonal eating to lower food emissions and low-carbon diets, gives a historical example of seasonal cooking shaped by four distinct seasons, and notes that apps now track what is in season.

## Why It Matters

Read the week before the [[seasonal-calendar-planning-2026-05-04]]. The useful idea was that peak flavour and harvest are not always the same moment, which matched what Stellan says about pears: better after a few weeks in store. We put "ask Stellan when it is best, not when it is picked" on the planning sheet. The emissions point went on a chalkboard line in June, briefly.

## Key Details

- Peak flavour usually coincides with harvest, with exceptions.
- Seasonal eating is linked in the article to lower greenhouse gas emissions from food.

## Caveats

General and not about baking or prices; for our seasons see [[seasonal-produce]].

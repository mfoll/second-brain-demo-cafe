# Bacillus cereus

## Summary

Wikipedia's article on Bacillus cereus, a rod-shaped, spore-forming bacterium common in soil and food. Some strains cause food poisoning; because the spores survive cooking, the risk comes from cooked food, especially starchy dishes such as rice, pasta and some soups, left warm for too long. Other strains are harmless or even used as animal probiotics.

## Why It Matters

Read in April 2026 when Mateus took over the soup and the menu review brought soup back from 2026-04-20 ([[menu-review-2026-04-09]]). Starchy soups made in bulk are exactly the kind of food this organism likes, and reheating does not undo toxins already formed. It is why the soup annex to the plan says reheat once only, and why soup is cooled in shallow containers like the fillings ([[two-stage-cooling]], [[food-safety-management-plan]]).

## Key Details

- Spore-former, survives cooking.
- Some strains produce toxins in food that are not destroyed by reheating.
- Classic risk: cooked starchy food cooled slowly or held warm.

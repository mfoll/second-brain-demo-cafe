# Croissant

## Summary

Wikipedia's article on the croissant: a crescent-shaped viennoiserie made from a laminated yeast dough, somewhere between bread and puff pastry. It covers the history of the shape and its spread, how the dough is made and the common variations, such as filled and almond croissants.

## Why It Matters

Read on the same evening as [[viennoiserie]], while preparing the [[price-review-2026]]. It reminded me why the croissant is costed as a laminated, butter-heavy product and not as "a bun": the yeast dough plus butter layers is the whole cost structure. Used for the short note on the counter card explaining what an all-butter croissant is ([[all-butter-croissants-are-not-where-we-save]]).

## Key Details

- Laminated yeast dough, so it carries both the bread costs (flour, yeast, proofing time) and the pastry costs (butter, lamination labour).
- Variations like the almond croissant use the same base, which is how we use up day-old croissants.

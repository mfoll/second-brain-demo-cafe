# Price Rises Once A Year, Not In Dribs

## Stance

Raise prices once a year, in one go, after a full recosting, and say so plainly. Do not move a few prices every few months.

## Scope

Counter, coffee and lunch. Christmas preorder prices and specials are set fresh each time and do not count.

## Why I Think This

- Regulars notice every change. One change a year with a short note ([[price-board-wording-2026]]) drew two comments in a week; the small rises of 2024 drew complaints for months.
- A full recost catches the items that have drifted quietly, not only the one whose supplier just wrote to us.
- It forces me to look at the whole menu once a year, which is when the lunch problem became visible.

## What Would Change My Mind

- An ingredient jump so big that one product loses money before the next review, for example butter above 10 EUR/kg.
- If customers turned out not to mind small rises; the 2026 week-one comments do not suggest that.

## Implications

The croissant stays at 2.95 until January 2027 unless butter breaks the line above ([[can-the-croissant-stay-at-2-95-eur]]). Fixing input prices ([[forward-buying]]) is the way to make once a year hold.

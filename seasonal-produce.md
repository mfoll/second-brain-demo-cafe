# Seasonal Produce

What is good, cheap and available each month, and how that shapes the specials board. Most of it comes through [[stellan-brook]] at Brook & Daughters; eggs from [[ada-quarrie]] at Quarrymoor Farm vary with the season too.

## How we use it

Specials are planned four weeks ahead in the [[seasonal-bake-calendar]] against Stellan's availability sheet ([[brook-and-daughters-seasonal-availability-sheet]]). A fruit goes on the calendar when it is both in season and under the price we set in the costing sheet, not just in season. Rhubarb in March and plums in September have been the best earners; strawberries in June are too dear for tarts after the wet springs.

## What the seasons look like for us

| Months | Good and cheap from Stellan | What we bake |
|---|---|---|
| March to May | Rhubarb, early herbs, spring greens | Rhubarb and custard tart, pea and mint soup |
| June to August | Strawberries (dear after a wet spring), apricots, courgettes, tomatoes, blackberries | Apricot Danish, tomato focaccia, courgette and feta scone |
| September to October | Plums, apples, squash, leeks | Plum and frangipane tart, apple and cheddar scone, squash soup |
| November to February | Pears, citrus, celeriac, chestnuts (dear) | Pear and ginger cake, chestnut bun, celeriac soup |

Eggs follow a season too: laying drops in late winter and Ada Quarrie cuts us to about 800 a week for two or three weeks ([[quarrymoor-farm-egg-supply-terms]], [[do-we-need-a-second-egg-supplier]]).

## Lessons so far

- Price beats romance. Strawberries at 7.50 EUR/kg made the June tart our weakest special at 58% margin.
- One ingredient, two uses. Apples went into the scone and the soup garnish; squash into soup and the pumpkin loaf. Less waste at 15:30.
- Stellan's warnings have been right every time so far. Read the "good but dear" column.

# Customer Deposits As Working Capital

Money taken from customers before we bake is cash we can use for the weeks when takings are low. For us it is the cheapest working capital there is: no interest, no bank, and it comes in exactly before our worst month.

## Where it comes from

The January 2025 squeeze: VAT of 11,800 EUR due on 31 January, a 3,400 EUR oven repair, quiet weeks after New Year, and the 6,000 EUR overdraft used in full for 19 days. The [[food-business-finance-course]] made me see it as a timing problem, not a profit problem.

## How we use it

- Christmas preorders take 30% at ordering. In 2025, 214 orders gave about 4,900 EUR of deposits; with the balances paid on collection, December cash covered most of the January 2026 dip and the overdraft was not used ([[christmas-2025-deposit-reconciliation]]).
- The deposits are booked as a liability until collection, not as sales. Ruth insists on it; the VAT falls due when the order is collected.
- In the [[thirteen-week-cash-flow-forecast]] deposits go in the week they are received, cautiously: only orders actually taken, never the target.

## Limits

- It is customers' money for goods we still owe. If we could not bake (oven failure, illness), we would owe it all back in a week.
- It works for Christmas and perhaps Easter. It is not a way to fund a second site.

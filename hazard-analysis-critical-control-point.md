# Hazard Analysis Critical Control Point

## Summary

The Wikipedia article on HACCP, the preventive approach to food safety that looks for biological, chemical and physical hazards in a process and puts controls where they can be prevented or reduced, instead of relying on testing the finished product. It sets out the usual seven principles: analyse hazards, find the critical control points, set critical limits, monitor, take corrective action, verify, and keep records.

## Why It Matters

I read this on 2025-01-14, the second day of the [[haccp-plan-rewrite]], to remind myself of the order of the principles before Kofi and I started mapping products on the wall. The point that stuck was that the method is about the process, not inspection of the result. The seventh principle, records, is the one we later failed on at the [[environmental-health-inspection-2025-06-18]].

## Key Details

- Seven principles, applied per product or process.
- Prerequisite programmes (cleaning, pest control, training) sit underneath and are not themselves critical control points.
- Originated in the space food programme and spread through food manufacturing; now the basis of most food safety management systems.

## Caveats

General background, not a guide to any particular regulator's expectations. Our limits come from our own plan ([[food-safety-management-plan]]), not from this article.

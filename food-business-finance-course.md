# Food Business Finance Course

The 12-week evening course in food business finance at Brackenmouth College, taught by [[farid-nasser]], and the break-even model I built for the shop as the final assignment.

## Outcome

Odile completes the 12-week evening course at Brackenmouth College and builds a break-even model for the shop. Reached on 8 December 2025.

## Current State

- Completed. Attendance 11 of 12 Mondays (missed 6 October, the evening I decided the wholesale pause).
- The output in use is [[break-even-model-workbook]]. It says the shop needs about 48,200 EUR of net sales a month to cover costs; 2025 averaged 50,800 EUR, a margin of safety of about 5%. That number is thin and it shaped how I read every second-site figure since.
- The model is updated each quarter after the VAT return and handed to [[ruth-marchbank]] with the year-end papers.

## Scope and Boundaries

The course and the assignment. The forecast itself belongs to [[cash-flow-forecast]] and [[thirteen-week-cash-flow-forecast]]; margins and prices to [[pricing-and-margins]]. The course fee was 480 EUR, paid by the company as training.

## Working Context

Why I signed up: after the January 2025 overdraft squeeze I could build a forecast but could not explain to the bank why a good month still left no cash. [[margot-elling]] suggested the course; Ruth agreed it would save her time.

What was useful ([[finance-course-syllabus]]):

- Separating fixed and variable costs properly. I had treated wages as variable; in a café with a fixed rota they are not. Moving them to fixed raised the break-even by about 9,000 EUR a month.
- [[contribution-margin]] by product line. Wholesale bread contributed far less per oven hour than anything in the shop, which I knew but had never put in numbers. It was part of the case for the pause ([[why-did-we-pause-wholesale-to-cafes]]).
- Working capital and timing: deposits, VAT and lease payments as timing problems rather than profit problems. That fed [[customer-deposits-as-working-capital]].

Less useful: the weeks on raising equity and on franchising.

The model, in short ([[break-even-takings]]): monthly fixed costs of about 31,800 EUR (rent, wages including employer costs, equipment lease, insurance, utilities, software, accountant), divided by an average contribution margin of 66% of net sales after ingredients, packaging and card fees. Farid's comment on the final presentation was that the 66% hides a wide spread between bakery and lunch; that point came back in the [[savoury-lunch-trim]].

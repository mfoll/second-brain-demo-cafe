# Food allergy

## Summary

Wikipedia's article on food allergy: an abnormal immune response to a food, with symptoms from itching and hives to swelling, vomiting, breathing difficulty and anaphylaxis, usually within minutes to a few hours. It covers the common trigger foods, diagnosis, avoidance as the main management, and the difference from food intolerance and food poisoning, which are not immune reactions.

## Why It Matters

I read it in the week after the walnut near miss ([[walnut-label-near-miss-2025-08-22]]) to make sure I could explain to the team why a missing word on a label is not a small error. Two points went into the [[allergen-training-2025-11-25]]: reactions can come fast and from small amounts, and an intolerance is not the same thing, so staff should not judge how serious a customer's request is.

## Key Details

- Peanuts, tree nuts, milk, eggs, wheat, soy, sesame, fish and shellfish are among the commonest triggers; most of them are on our bench every day.
- Avoidance depends on accurate information, which is why labels come only from the [[allergen-matrix]].
- Reactions can be severe even when a previous one was mild.

## Caveats

Medical background only. It does not tell a bakery how to label; our approach is in [[precautionary-allergen-labelling]] and [[do-we-need-may-contain-labels]].

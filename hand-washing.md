# Hand washing

## Summary

Wikipedia's article on hand washing, or hand hygiene: cleaning the hands with soap and water to remove microorganisms and dirt, with drying as part of the process because wet hands pick up contamination more easily. Hand sanitiser is described as a substitute only when hands are not visibly dirty or greasy. The article reviews evidence that regular hand washing reduces the spread of infections.

## Why It Matters

I read it two days after the inspection that found our basement hand-wash sink blocked by flour sacks ([[environmental-health-inspection-2025-06-18]]). Two points went into the briefing for the team: drying matters, so paper towels are on the opening check, and sanitiser does not replace washing in a bakery where hands are floury and buttery all morning. The sink rule (nothing within a metre) came from the same week ([[basement-storage-changes-2025]], [[daily-food-safety-checks]]).

## Key Details

- Soap and water, then thorough drying.
- Sanitiser is less effective on greasy or dirty hands.
- Regular washing reduces the spread of infection in everyday settings.

## Caveats

Public-health overview; not specific to food businesses.

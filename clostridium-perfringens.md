# Clostridium perfringens

## Summary

Wikipedia's article on Clostridium perfringens, a spore-forming bacterium found widely in soil, decaying vegetation and the gut. Its spores can survive cooking, and it grows extremely fast in the right conditions; the article cites one of the shortest generation times recorded for any organism in laboratory culture. It is a common cause of food poisoning linked to meat dishes and food cooked in bulk then left to cool slowly.

## Why It Matters

This was the article that made cooling click for Kofi. Cooking kills most bacteria, but not these spores, and a deep pot of chicken or soup cooling slowly on a bench gives them hours to grow. It is the reason soup now goes into four shallow containers and the reason a missed 2-hour reading means the batch is thrown away ([[how-do-we-cool-cooked-fillings-safely]], [[two-stage-cooling]]).

## Key Details

- Spore-former: survives normal cooking temperatures.
- Grows rapidly at warm temperatures; slow cooling of bulk food is the classic failure.
- Associated with meat, poultry, gravies and stews.

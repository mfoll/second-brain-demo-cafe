# Foodborne illness

## Summary

Wikipedia's overview of illness caused by contaminated food: bacteria, viruses, parasites and toxins, how they get into food, typical incubation times and symptoms, and the main prevention measures (cooking, cooling, cold holding, hand hygiene and separating raw from ready-to-eat food). It notes that much of the harm comes from the body's response to the agent, which varies between people.

## Why It Matters

I read it the weekend after the June 2025 inspection, trying to explain to the team why a missing cooling record mattered so much when the food itself was fine ([[environmental-health-inspection-2025-06-18]]). The useful part for us was the list of preventive habits, which maps almost one for one onto our critical control points and prerequisites. I used it for the September 2025 training session on cooling and reheating ([[front-of-house-training-plan-2026]] repeated it in 2026).

## Key Details

- Many illnesses have incubation periods of hours to days, so a customer rarely links a meal to the illness, and a business rarely hears about it.
- Spore-forming bacteria that survive cooking are a particular risk in food cooked in bulk and cooled slowly ([[two-stage-cooling]]).
- Hand washing and keeping ill staff off food handling are among the simplest controls ([[staff-handbook]]).

## Caveats

A broad public-health overview; it says nothing about how a small bakery should set its limits. Our limits are in the [[food-safety-management-plan]].

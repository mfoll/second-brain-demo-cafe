# Lessons From The Oven Failure

Written on a Monday in December 2025, once the new oven was in and I could think about it calmly.

1. **The warning was in the repair bills.** 4,290 EUR of repairs in seven months of 2025 on a second-hand oven from 2019 ([[old-deck-oven-service-history]]). After the May element I should have asked for quotes. I waited for it to stop.
2. **Know where a replacement comes from before you need one.** Lead times ran from 6 to 14 weeks ([[deck-oven-quotes-2025]]). We spent three and a half months on a hired oven at 1,150 EUR a month.
3. **A hired oven protects the shop, not everything.** It ran at about 70% of our capacity. Something had to give, and it was wholesale ([[why-did-we-pause-wholesale-to-cafes]]).
4. **Check the building first.** The new oven needed a three-phase supply that took a week to arrange with Cyril Ashdown and the electrician.
5. **The hours go up before anyone says so.** Kofi did his longest week of the year on one deck ([[early-shift-overtime-review-2025-09-02]]).

What I changed: an [[equipment-register]] with a watch list, and a standing rule that any piece of essential equipment with two repairs in a year gets a replacement quote.

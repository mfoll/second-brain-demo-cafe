# Proofing (baking technique)

## Summary

Article on proofing, or proving: the final rest and rise of a yeast dough before baking, when the yeast ferments and produces the gas that leavens it. It also covers retarding dough in the cold, proofing cabinets, and how to tell under- from over-proofed dough.

## Why It Matters

Read after Colm's service of the retarder-proofer ([[proofer-service-report-2026-02]]), when Kofi and I talked about what would happen if the cabinet drifted two degrees warm overnight. The retard is what lets the sourdough be shaped at 19:30 and baked at 04:45; a warm cabinet means over-proofed loaves before anyone is in. It also comes up in the [[central-production-kitchen]] discussion, since a longer retard is one way round a 03:00 start.

## Key Details

- Proofing is the last rise, after shaping; bulk fermentation comes before it.
- Cold retarding slows the yeast so the timing can be moved, and adds flavour.

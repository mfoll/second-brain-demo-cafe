# Maillard reaction

## Summary

The browning reaction between amino acids and reducing sugars that gives crust, toast and roasted foods their colour and much of their flavour. It runs quickly from about 140 to 165 °C; higher up, caramelisation and then burning take over.

## Why It Matters

After the [[flour-bake-trial-2025-05-14]] the Tarnwick loaves browned faster and darker at the same oven setting. Kofi's explanation was more free sugars from the longer, warmer ferment and more protein in the flour, which is the reaction this article describes. We lowered the bottom deck by 10 degrees for the first week of July 2025 rather than shorten the bake.

## Key Details

- Proceeds quickly from about 140 to 165 °C; above that, caramelisation and burning take over.
- Same chemistry as coffee roasting, which made it a useful link for Amira's training ([[coffee-roasting]]).

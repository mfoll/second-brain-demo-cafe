# Menu engineering

## Summary

Wikipedia's article on designing a menu for profit, an approach introduced by Michael Kasavana and Donald Smith in 1982. Much of it is about where items sit on a printed menu and what diners notice, the "sweet spots" idea, which the article says lacks empirical support. It also notes that descriptive item names may raise perceived value, and covers cost-margin analysis, which picks out items with low food cost and above-average gross profit.

## Why It Matters

Read on the Sunday after the [[menu-review-2026-04-09]], trying to understand how I had got the lunch cut wrong. The useful part for us was cost-margin analysis: it ranks items by the gross profit they bring in, not by food cost percentage alone. A 6.80 EUR soup at 64% margin brings in more cash per sale than a 3.40 EUR bun at 76%. I had compared percentages. The lesson went into [[lunch-should-stay-small-but-not-disappear]].

## Key Details

- Founders and date: Kasavana and Smith, 1982.
- Placement "sweet spots" are popular but unproven, so we did not redesign the board around them.
- Descriptive names: we did rename the soup on the board to say what is in it each day.

## Caveats

Written for restaurant menus; a bakery counter is mostly bought by eye. Nothing in it measures what else a customer buys with an item, which was our actual blind spot.

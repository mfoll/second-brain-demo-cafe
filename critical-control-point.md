# Critical control point

## Summary

Short Wikipedia article defining a critical control point as a step where a control can be applied and a food safety hazard prevented, eliminated or reduced to an acceptable level. It gives cooking as the most common example and stresses that each point needs a measurable limit.

## Why It Matters

Kofi and I argued for an afternoon about whether allergen segregation was a critical control point. This article, and the HACCP one, settled it for us: a critical control point needs a step where a measurable limit removes or reduces the hazard. Nothing in a one-room bakery removes an allergen, so allergen control became a prerequisite with its own records ([[food-safety-management-plan]], [[allergen-management]]). We ended with four CCPs.

## Key Details

- A CCP is a step, not a product.
- Each needs a critical limit, monitoring and a corrective action ([[critical-limit]]).
- Too many CCPs dilute attention; a short list monitored well is the goal.

## Caveats

A stub-length article with thin sourcing. Used only to settle terminology.

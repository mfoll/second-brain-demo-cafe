# A Food Hall Unit Beats A Standalone Shop

## Stance

For our first second site, a unit in a food hall is better than a standalone shop: the hall provides the expensive, slow parts (extraction, seating, toilets, waste, footfall) and we provide bread, pastry and coffee.

## Scope

A second site for a small business like ours, with one owner. Not a general view about food halls.

## Why I Think This

- [[tanners-row-site]] needed 38,000 EUR of extraction and flue before a single croissant ([[tanners-row-extraction-and-flue-quote]]).
- Unit 7 comes with extraction and seating for 1,450 EUR plus 310 EUR service charge a month ([[saltings-market-hall-unit-7]]).
- Shared seating means fewer staff per cover.

## Why I Am Revising It

Status changed to Revising after the feasibility work. The hall's advantages are real, but its risk is concentrated: footfall depends on someone else's project, which has not opened, and the projection comes from the landlord ([[saltings-hall-footfall-projections]]). The hall's coffee kiosk 25 m away competes on our best-margin line ([[saltings-hall-tenant-mix-plan]]). A standalone shop is costlier to open but its fate is ours.

## What Would Change My Mind

Back to Active: six months of the hall trading at or above two-thirds of its projection. Toward the opposite view: a standalone food premises with existing extraction at a similar rent.

## Implications

For now, unit 7 remains the only candidate; I am not looking for another shop.

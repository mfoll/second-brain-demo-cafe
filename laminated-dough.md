# Laminated dough

## Summary

Short article on dough made of many thin layers separated by solid fat, built up by folding and rolling. In the oven the water in the butter turns to steam and lifts the layers while the fat crisps them, which is where the flake comes from. Laminated doughs can have well over eighty layers.

## Why It Matters

I sent it to Nils Jorgensen before his visit so we were talking about the same thing: what matters in a lamination butter is how it behaves cold and folded, not how it tastes on toast. It framed the test in [[second-dairy-tasting-2026-10-14]] and Priyanka's notes in [[priyanka-email-butter-sheet-trial-2026-10]].

## Key Details

- Steam from the butter's water does the lifting, so water content and plasticity of the butter matter.
- The fat has to stay solid between layers through the folds; a butter that softens fast smears and the layers fuse.

## Caveats

No numbers on butter fat content or working temperatures; those come from the dairies' spec sheets ([[second-dairy-product-specifications-2026-10]]).

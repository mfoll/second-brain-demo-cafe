# Paper Check Sheets Beat Apps In The Kitchen

## Stance

For our food safety checks, a one-page paper sheet on a clipboard works better than a tablet app. Deputy is right for the rota; the probe readings stay on paper.

## Scope

A single site with one basement and nine staff. Not the rota, timesheets or allergen data, which belong in software.

## Why I think this

- Floury, wet hands at 04:30. A clipboard by the door gets filled in; a tablet in a case gets put off until later, and "later" is how readings get invented.
- The sheet shows the whole day at a glance, so a missing cooling reading jumps out at the next person.
- Imogen Hale read our sheets in minutes at the re-rating visit ([[re-rating-visit-2025-10-07]]).
- A sales rep demonstrated a food safety app with Bluetooth probes in November 2025: 69 EUR a month, plus probes.

## What would change my mind

A second site. Reviewing sheets from two places on a Monday would not work, and wireless fridge sensors with alerts would have caught the chiller failure overnight instead of at 04:35 ([[walk-in-chiller-failure-2026-01-27]]).

## Implications

Keep paper for now. Price wireless fridge sensors as part of any [[central-production-kitchen]] plan.

# Two-Stage Cooling

Cooling cooked food in two timed stages: fast through the warmest range where bacteria grow quickest, then down to fridge temperature. Our version: from the end of cooking to below 20 °C within 2 hours, then below 5 °C within a further 4 hours.

## Why two stages

The danger is not being warm, it is staying warm. Spore-forming bacteria survive cooking and multiply fastest roughly between body temperature and the 20s ([[clostridium-perfringens]], [[danger-zone-food-safety]]). Getting through that band quickly matters more than the final few degrees.

## What makes it work in our basement

- Shallow trays, 5 cm deep at most; soup split into four containers.
- Uncovered on the rack by the extractor until 20 °C, then covered and into the walk-in.
- Times written on the tray label so the next person knows when to check.

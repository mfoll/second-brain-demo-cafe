# Dough Hydration

Water as a percentage of flour weight in a dough, the baker's percentage. 76% means 760 g of water per kg of flour, counting the water and flour in the levain.

## Why it matters to us

Hydration decides how the dough handles, how open the crumb is and how long the loaf keeps. It is also the first thing that moves when the flour changes: the Tarnwick stone-ground flour needed 76% to feel like the old roller-milled flour at 72% ([[flour-bake-trial-2025-05-14]]), because finer bran and germ take up more water ([[flour-extraction-rate]]).

## Our numbers

- House sourdough: 76%, dropped by one or two points for two weeks after each new harvest ([[tarnwick-new-harvest-adjustment-2025-09-23]]).
- Seeded rye: 82%.
- Focaccia: 80%.
- Croissant dough: about 52%, with the butter counted separately.

## Watch out

A higher hydration is not "better bread" by itself. Kofi's rule: change it by one point at a time and only after two bakes.

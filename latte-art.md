# Latte art

## Summary

Short article on pouring microfoam into espresso to make a pattern on the surface of a latte, cappuccino or hot chocolate, or drawing one in the foam afterwards. It needs espresso with crema so the white foam shows against it. The article traces the technique from Italy and its spread in the 1980s and 1990s, including the rosette pattern.

## Why It Matters

Grace sent it round the day after the [[ombrel-barista-training-2025-11-25]], where she turned out to pour the best hearts of any of us. It made the point Dev had made: the pattern only works with good crema and properly textured milk, so it is a quick visual check on the shot and the steaming, not decoration. We now ask for a simple heart or tulip on every flat white and use a failed pattern as a reason to check the grinder.

## Key Details

- Depends on espresso crema and microfoam meeting at the surface.
- Free pouring (moving the jug) and etching (drawing afterwards) are the two methods; we only free pour.

## Caveats

No guidance on milk temperature or training; for those see [[espresso-dial-in-notes]].
